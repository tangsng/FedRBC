# 6. Discussion and Limitations

## 6.1 Why composition beats any single defense family

The evaluation shows a consistent pattern (Table 4): each baseline does best in exactly the attack geometry its own mechanism targets, and worst everywhere else. FedGT's hard identification cleanly separates direction-inconsistent clients at low attack intensity, but it is brittle — a binary threshold either drops the adversary outright or lets it through at full weight, which shows up as widening seed variance. EIFFeL's coordinate consensus handles moderate direction attacks yet collapses once the malicious set becomes the plurality. RAID's per-round scoring has no memory, so it cannot converge to a decision. What sets FedRBC apart is not a stronger version of any single family but the way the pieces work together: the softmax gate provides what binary identification cannot (graded, low-variance suppression), the root reference provides what plurality cannot (collusion resistance), relative clipping provides what direction cannot (magnitude resistance), and the ledger provides what per-round scoring cannot (memory). The threat-coverage matrix (Table 3) predicts the outcome table (Table 4) row by row — the clearest sign that the result reflects the architecture rather than chance. The published system closest to ours — the blockchain-SDN edge framework of Tyagi et al. (CMC 2026) [G2] — shares the same three-layer setting but treats security only through qualitative discussion: no dataset, no runnable poisoning evaluation, and no robustness guarantee for the learning process. The emerging fielded deployments — blockchain-secured FL for industrial air-quality monitoring [N23] and the three-hospital FL-IoT installation of [N24] — show that the architecture class is deployable in practice, yet neither system can isolate a convicted device from the network, which is precisely the operational step FedRBC adds. FedRBC keeps the architectural idea (chain as trust anchor, SDN as actuator) but moves the evaluation to where the claims actually live: three attack classes, three recent aggregation systems, and theorem-level bounds for convergence, suppression, and containment.

## 6.2 Lessons from the design space

Two instructive failures encountered during development delimit where reputation-composed defenses do *not* help. (i) *Soft power gating is a trap.* Replacing a hard gate by $\max(0, s_i)^p$ with $p < 1$ — intended to retain benign-but-skewed updates under non-IID — consistently degraded accuracy under attack (up to 14 F1 points at $\beta=0.3$), because whatever softens the gate for skewed honest updates softens it for poison in exactly the same way; the softmax form of Eq. (6) was adopted precisely because it distinguishes the two by *degree* (exponential suppression of reversal, $O(1)$ retention of near-zero evidence). (ii) *Fixed clipping radii misfire against magnitude attacks.* An early version with constant $\tau_c$ lost to a plain median/MAD filter under scaling, because a direction-preserving magnitude attack is invisible to cosine evidence; making the radius relative to $\|\Delta_{\mathrm{root}}\|$ (Eq. 5) closed precisely this hole. The composition cannot compensate for a missing physical mechanism.

## 6.3 Limitations

Five limitations bound the claims. First, the evaluation is simulation-based: attacks are injected by the harness rather than captured from live compromise; while this is standard practice for controlled Byzantine evaluation, attacker creativity in the wild will exceed even the expanded set evaluated here (direction-inconsistent forgeries, LIE-style collusive perturbations, targeted backdoors, and reference poisoning — Section 5.9), and the measured backdoor results show the boundary concretely: main-task F1 is preserved but attack success is not suppressed by any evaluated method. Deployment feasibility, for its part, is bounded by the compute envelope measured in Section 5.6: FedRBC's client-side cost is local SGD plus one cosine against the broadcast root direction — no on-device cryptography — and the trust computation is a fused tensor pass of the same order as plain averaging, so the mechanism fits the sensor class the evaluation emulates; the 20-client emulation itself (Section 5.1) makes no device-feasibility claim beyond this envelope, and the N=50/100 scaling study shows the variance only improves with population. Second, the root set is a trust assumption, and its limits are empirically visible: under extreme non-IID ($\alpha=0.1$) with 30% of updates forged, FedRBC's F1 drops by 24 points to $0.326$ (Fig. 6) — honest directions scatter so widely that directional evidence loses contrast, and the residual performance reflects the reference degrading rather than failing. Reference poisoning is now measured empirically (Section 5.9) rather than assumed away, but fully adversarial references remain an open problem shared with all trust-bootstrapped defenses [B3], mitigated by quarterly refresh and, prospectively, rotating cross-validated root subsets. Third, the MTTC analysis models the event pipeline as M/M/1; bursty, heavy-tailed alert arrivals will inflate the tail beyond the mean we report, and a GI/G/1 refinement is future work. The same architectural separation bounds controller failure: if the ONOS controller is unavailable, containment actuation degrades, but the learning path never does — aggregation-side defences (gate, clipping, Layer-0) keep training, and quarantine decisions replay from the immutable ledger once the controller returns, which is the containment-side counterpart of the same asymmetry. Fourth, FedRBC protects raw data by construction, and the accounted output-perturbation layer of Section 4.7 protects the aggregated trajectory against gradient inversion with an explicit RDP budget — the accounting builds on the Rényi-differential-privacy composition calculus of Mironov [D1], while the privacy–robustness trade-off we measure in Section 5.9 extends the DP-in-FL performance analysis of Wei et al. [D2] from honest averaging to the robust-aggregation setting, and the same budget that bounds membership-inference on the released trajectory is therefore explicit rather than assumed; the residual boundary is per-update confidentiality against an honest-but-curious aggregation server, which requires secure aggregation and remains future work. Fifth, as the measurements themselves surfaced: grey-zone penalization is not noise-aware. As update norms shrink late in training, directional evidence becomes noise-dominated and erodes *all* reputations, honest ones included (53% of honest clients touch the quarantine line by round 60 in the attack runs; 85-100% over 60 no-attack rounds — Section 5.5). The relative ranking survives, the F1 defence is unaffected, and cold-start probation provides the recovery path — a quarantined device re-enters at $r_0$ under the documented probation of Sections 4.4–4.5 — but a deployment that reads $r_{\mathrm{q}}$ as an absolute no-false-quarantine guarantee would over-isolate; a penalty gate that widens the grey zone as $\|\Delta_{\mathrm{root}}\|$ shrinks is the direct fix. Finally, a clinical-safety note for the intended environment: containment isolates a convicted device's *training traffic* from the federation, not its clinical function — an infusion pump keeps infusing, a monitor keeps monitoring; for equipment whose participation is nonetheless deemed critical, the contract's cold-start path (rather than indefinite quarantine) and read-only fallback modes keep the device re-admissible without model influence, so the operational risk of isolation is a bounded re-entry delay rather than a clinical outage. Future work also includes live-traffic validation with red-team exercises and root-set hardening through rotating, cross-validated root subsets.

# 7. Conclusion

This paper introduced FedRBC, a blockchain-enabled reputation-weighted robust federated learning framework for healthcare IoT edge networks. Its organizing principle is a single on-chain trust root that drives aggregation, selection, and containment together. A reference-guided aggregation rule — Layer-0 statistical pre-filtering, relative magnitude clipping, and softmax evidence gating — covers the direction-inconsistent attack classes that defeat plurality-based defenses, with an accounted differential-privacy option whose privacy–robustness trade-off is measured; a smart-contract reputation ledger adds trust memory, immutability, and auditability; and an SDN-actuated loop turns on-chain conviction into network quarantine within a bounded mean time. The theory is deliberately mechanistic: a new lemma bounds the residual malicious weight of the convergence result *explicitly* in the gate's directional separation, the ledger's reputation ratio, and the temperature, rather than assuming it away. A simulation-based evaluation on a containerized testbed — ten-seed main-table replication, two datasets, extended attack families (LIE, backdoors with attack-success-rate scoring, reference poisoning), Byzantine ratios to 50%, and client populations to 100, anchored by centralized and clean-FedAvg references with Holm-corrected significance testing — showed FedRBC attaining the best AUC and the smallest variance in every scenario, an F1 statistically tied with the strongest classical baseline, FLTrust, and significantly ahead of every plurality- or coordinate-based baseline where those families fail (Sybil collusion), while being the only evaluated method that carries trust memory, auditability, and network-level containment; it stays above 0.5 F1 at a 50% Byzantine ratio and holds its defence when the reference set itself is poisoned (a Sybil-regime result, Section 5.9). The system's limitations are stated plainly: simulation-based attacks, a trusted root set (now measured rather than assumed), a mean-field queueing model, evidence degeneration late in training that erodes absolute reputations, and backdoor resistance outside the mechanism's scope. Future work includes live-traffic validation, bursty-arrival containment analysis, root-set hardening, a noise-aware penalty gate, and secure aggregation for per-update confidentiality.
